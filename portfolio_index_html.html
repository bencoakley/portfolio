Pratt & Whitney — Robotic Deburring Cell

Representative FANUC deburring video shown because actual cell footage and parts are ITAR-controlled.

I currently support seven robotic deburring cells used in aerospace manufacturing. My work includes supporting FANUC robots, improving robotic deburring paths for complex part shapes, optimizing cycle time, and helping make the process more repeatable.

I also work on improvements around end-of-arm tooling, process parameters, and how parts are delivered into the cell. Also, a big part of my role is making clear work instructions so operators can run the process consistently.

Tennibot — Manufacturing Engineer

At Tennibot, I supported the manufacturing and assembly of the Tennibot Rover, an autonomous robot designed to collect tennis balls. My work focused on making the assembly process more repeatable, quicker, and easier to confirm quality through testing.

I created work instructions for subassemblies, designed assembly jigs in SolidWorks, built testing fixtures, and wrote Python programs to test sensor subassemblies. This role gave me hands-on experience with the full manufacturing process, from design support to assembly, testing, troubleshooting, and documentation.

Additional Projects

Tennibot — Mechanical Design

I worked on updating the design of the Tennibot Rover’s collection arm. The goal was to create an arm that could collect tennis balls along the net and chain-link fence while also being simple enough to manufacture and assemble.

The project involved balancing performance, sensor feedback, and manufacturability. It was a good example of designing a part that had to work in the real world.

Auburn University Small Satellite Program

I was part of Auburn University’s Small Satellite Program, where my role was to help model the satellite assembly, maintain the CAD structure, and document design changes as the project developed.

This required communicating with project teams, team leads, and sponsors to make sure the model stayed accurate and useful through reviews, documentation, and project milestones.

Metal Shop Project

I helped a local metal art business improve the manufacturability of one of their Auburn themed products. The original design required detailed hand painting, which made it difficult and time consuming to produce consistently.

I redesigned the product as a layered metal assembly so each layer could be cut, painted, and assembled more easily. I also created the 2D CAD files used to cut the parts on a CNC plasma table.
